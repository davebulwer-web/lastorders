BUILD NOTES

Every game is a module that honours one contract — init, update, render, press, settled, result — so the shell never knows what it is running. Adding the next one is a new file, not a new engine.

The playfield renders at 480×270 (16:9, an exact 4× of 1080p) and scales up by whole numbers with nearest-neighbour, so every sprite is drawn at its exact pixel size, never scaled:

BALL 14×14 SKITTLE 10×24 POST 12×54 PINT 11×17 ROOM 480×270 COIN 16×16 BULL WALL 480×270

Opponents play the real game, turn about, on the same physics as you. Before each shot they try a few hundred lines and weights on a copy of the table, keep the one whose neighbours also score (so a small slip still pays), then play it with a character's own amount of error. Making someone harder or kinder is two numbers.

Art lives in art/ as PNGs drawn 1:1 (made with PixelLab). The room backdrop is the shell's layer: every game draws it first, and the page shows it again, blurred, behind everything. Anything missing falls back to the placeholder blocks.

Ring the Bull is the one game in true 3D: a ring on a 1.6m string from the ceiling, swinging on a sphere, seen from behind your shoulder. The hook stands 6cm off the wall, so the ring has to reach it with the hole lined up, and arrive slowly enough to drop on rather than bang the wall and jump off. Held back and let go, a pendulum keeps to its line, so where you point it is where it meets the wall.

Physics runs on a flat top-down plane; the view is a 3/4 squash of 0.55 on the vertical, which is why the ball's circular swing reads as an ellipse. Sprites are anchored at the base and drawn back to front.

The ball starts at full stretch, so the chain can only ever take away its outward speed — swing it sideways and all that pace survives. The post is solid and the chain is shorter than the distance to the pins, so going round it is the only route. Every radian of swing winds a little chain onto the post, bringing the ball in a ring at a time: the back row on the first pass, the middle on the second, the front on the third if there's still life in it.
